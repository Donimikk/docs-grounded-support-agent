# "Are you faster than the others?"

This comes from people still deciding whether to buy a subscription. It is not a
gap that needs escalating - we have the answer, the two questions being asked at
once simply need separating.

**"How are you different from other bots?" the documentation does answer.**
`home_faq.md` has exactly that question and answers it in terms of form, not
numbers: Fyndit runs inside Discord, no application has to be installed, alerts
and actions arrive where the person already is, and there is a real free plan.
That can be said plainly and it is more concrete than anything about speed.

**"Are you faster?" the documentation no longer answers.** There is no
measurement against specific bots in the corpus. (The marketing "blazing fast"
from the same page is not a measurement and is not quoted as a fact.)

## What can be said about speed

It depends on how each system "feels" on a given day. **On average we are not
slower and we are often faster.**

The second half belongs with it immediately, because without it the first is
advertising: **nobody has good measurements, because no server publishes its
statistics.** This is not left out. It is honest, it sounds more credible than a
claim of victory, and it tells the customer what to make of numbers from anyone.

## What must not be said

- **No numbers.** Not milliseconds, not "X% faster". We do not measure it.
- **No claim about a specific competitor's performance.** We know nothing about
  another product that we could back up.
- **No promise that you will get the item.** Speed is not a guarantee of a
  purchase - only one person can buy one thing anyway.

## The owner's claim is his, not our measurement

In a ticket the owner said more: that outsourced bots run with a delay on a
two-year-old bot that barely receives updates, while Fyndit now has its own
exclusive bot faster than any outsourced one - and that even an outsourced Fyndit
is faster than a named competitor.

This is **the owner's claim about his own product**, not a measured result. It
can be referred to in the shape it has ("according to the owner…"), but it does
not move into the facts. A helper should not repeat it as their own finding.

The owner also sent a link to a status and speed page at the time - we do not
have the address yet, so it is not linked.

## A question about an account ban is something else

It arrives together with speed in one message, but **the documentation does
answer it** and it must not be marked as a gap:

- `home_faq.md` - "Is autocop allowed on Vinted?" → every user is responsible for
  how they use the tool, in line with the platform's terms
- `cgu.md`, `cgv.md` - the user is solely responsible for their settings,
  sessions and automations; Fyndit is independent and not affiliated with Vinted

So the right answer is: **there is no guarantee**, Fyndit is an independent tool,
Vinted can change its rules and the responsibility is the user's. Only after that
can the experience be added that most people have had nothing happen to their
main account.

Watch out for two sentences that look like the answer and are not: `cgu.md` on
**third parties** being able to restrict **their own services**, and `cgu.md` on
**Fyndit** being able to suspend **access to Fyndit**. Neither is about a Vinted
account being banned.

## When this note does NOT apply

The stance about responsibility belongs to the question **"can I get banned?"**
asked in general - typically by somebody still deciding whether to buy.

**When a customer reports a specific error that the documentation explains,
answer with that explanation.** For example "Access denied" during autocop:
according to `docs_autocop.md` Vinted temporarily restricts the account from
buying for **about 24 hours**, usually after too many attempts or cancelled
purchases in a short time. That is the answer the person needs.

Measured: asked that question, the model retold the whole paragraph but **left
out the 24 hours** and replaced them with a sentence about guaranteeing nothing.
The sentence was true and worthless in that place - the customer did not want a
legal position, they wanted to know how long it would last.

A general caveat can be added after a specific answer. Never instead of it.
